How the reel command & PWM flashing work
Runs as a live integrating loop, same as the firmware's per-cycle P-controller, so it ramps toward the target rather than jumping. Gain is 0.02 normally, 0.03 when reversing direction, 0.04 when decelerating into reverse, per control.cpp. Lateral (X) deflection only ever biases toward unwind, only Y can drive wind, and it's a narrow margin against the 0.10 discard threshold, that asymmetry is real, not a sim bug.

UNREEL / REEL IN / HOLD is derived from the firmware's two real digital outputs, DO_Motor_Unwind and DO_Motor_Wind. HOLD isn't its own signal, it's the implicit state when both are low, same as the hardware. The active states flash at the real PWM period (1000 ms, Deflection_Trim_PWMPeriod_ms) with on-time equal to the duty cycle, so a low duty blinks briefly and 100% sits solid, that flashing is the actual output waveform rather than an animation. Reaching a non-HOLD state at all needs the published vector out of its deadzone first, X past 18° or Y past 63°, then the ramp has to climb past the 0.10 discard threshold.

How turns/inertia/tension are modeled
Turns-per-layer is width ÷ cable OD (1.835m / 0.0538m ≈ wraps); each layer's radius grows by one cable OD outward from the core, so length-per-turn increases per layer — the naive "turns × circumference" shortcut the council flagged as the false-sense-of-safety failure mode is NOT what this uses. Total capacity, worked back from the real 359.4m spec through this same model, comes out to turns across layers, reconciling to m.

This box is informational only: the sim has no tramming/drive feature to actually gate, it's a live readout of what a real interlock built this way would report and whether it would currently allow tramming. The rearm margin exists because a single hard threshold chatters right at the boundary and gets routed around by operators — exactly the nuisance-lockout failure mode the council flagged.

Turn count is no longer a canned duty-proportional rate (that was the original, admittedly unphysical placeholder) — it's now integrated from real torque vs. load each frame: net torque = (duty% × motor stall torque × gear ratio × efficiency, read live from the Motor & drum torque check panel below) combined with a cable-tension load torque (tension × current wrap radius, defaulting to the 200 lbf the vendor sheet recommends), divided by I(L) from the same drum-inertia model as that panel, integrated over real elapsed time (dt from performance.now(), clamped to 0.1s so a backgrounded tab doesn't produce a huge catch-up jump on return). This is why loading the "Sandvik reel motor" preset below now visibly changes how fast the drum spins up, and why a heavily spooled drum (high I) accelerates more sluggishly than a nearly empty one at the same duty — that lag didn't exist before.

Tension is modeled asymmetrically (fixed 2026-08-06, after verification showed the real Sandvik motor spec vs. real 200 lbf tension couldn't overcome each other in EITHER direction under the earlier symmetric model, so unwind could never start at all): tension has a fixed physical direction, toward unwind, since it's caused by the rig pulling cable off the drum, not a friction-like force that flips sign with the drum's current motion. During REEL IN it opposes the motor as a real load (and if tension wins, the drum gets dragged into unwinding despite the wind command — a real failure mode, not clamped away). During UNREEL it now ASSISTS the commanded torque instead of resisting it, since a real hydraulic unreel is more a controlled release than an active drive. During HOLD, a stationary drum is still treated as fully held against tension (no counterbalance/holding-valve spec exists to model a leak or creep rate, and real hydraulic reel drives normally include exactly that kind of valve for this reason) — there's also no separate friction term. Both left as open items rather than invented numbers. Max drum speed is a hard cap from the vendor sheet's flow-limited peak reel RPM, since the torque model itself has no speed dependence (accurate near stall per the existing back-EMF caveat below, not accurate near the real speed limit without this cap).

How this torque check works
Drum inertia I(L) uses the same closed-form model as scripts/reel_inertia_model.py (continuous-annulus approximation, verified there to agree with the discrete layer-by-layer method to <0.1%), on the real Sandvik drum geometry (62in core, 72.25in width, 72in flange) and the real Nexans 36-517-002 cable spec. That geometry is real. The three fields below (motor stall torque, gear ratio, drivetrain efficiency) default to round placeholder numbers so the mechanism is visible even with nothing real entered — but a real spec now exists, "Load Sandvik reel motor" below fills them from a real vendor calc sheet (hydraulic_calcs, 2026-08-06): a hydraulic reel motor driving the drum through a 22:32 sprocket chain, theoretical torque/speed calculated from peak circuit pressure and motor displacement (the sheet also has a real Eaton vendor spec-sheet column, which runs higher since it reflects real motor losses an ideal calc doesn't capture — theoretical was chosen as the more conservative default). One open item from that sheet: its cable diameter (2.2in) doesn't quite match the 2.12in Nexans spec the drum/capacity-interlock model above uses (~4% off, not rounding noise) — unreconciled, flagged for whoever can confirm which cable spec is authoritative. Available torque at the drum shaft is computed as duty% × stall torque × gear ratio × efficiency (a stall-torque model: assumes PWM duty maps linearly to average torque at zero/low speed, no back-EMF correction). This box exists to show HOW that comparison works and make the missing inputs obvious, not to assert whether 12% duty is enough — it isn't, until real numbers replace the placeholders above.

"Test a specific duty cycle" decouples this box from the sliders above, so you can ask "what would 50% duty do" without having to actually drive the encoders to whatever angle happens to produce 50%. Off (default), it reads the reel trim loop's live duty every frame, same as before.

The 5 HP hydraulic preset fills in an illustrative stall torque computed from power and an assumed rated speed (torque = power ÷ ω, 5 HP = 3728.5 W at an assumed 500 RPM → ~71 Nm), plus a modest gear ratio and efficiency for a close-coupled hydraulic drive. The 500 RPM is an assumption, not a datasheet number — no real hydraulic motor spec exists either. Also worth knowing: a hydraulic motor doesn't have back-EMF at all, so unlike the DC case, its real torque stays roughly flat across its speed range instead of tapering to zero at some no-load RPM — that specific caveat from the electric-motor model doesn't apply here, though the duty%-as-average-torque approximation still does, since it depends on how the real DO_Motor_Wind/DO_Motor_Unwind outputs actually drive the hydraulics (a time-proportioned valve vs. something else isn't confirmed).
